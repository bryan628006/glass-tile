# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Professionals and knowledge workers who use Tiles as part of a creative or technical workflow. They are building or constructing something — the tool is a means to an end. They expect precision, speed, and a UI that stays out of their way.

## Product Purpose

Tiles is a creation and building tool. Users construct output through a tile- or block-based interface. Success looks like: the user produces something meaningful without friction, the tool feels fast and capable, and the output matches the user's intent without unnecessary mediation.

## Brand Personality

Precise. Quiet. Capable.

Tiles should feel like a professional instrument — the kind of tool that earns trust through consistency and restraint. No decorative excess, no cheerfulness that undermines credibility. Developer-grade confidence without being cold.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS templates — Bootstrap grids, Material Design defaults, stock hero sections with blue gradients. Tiles should feel designed, not assembled. Every spacing decision, every component, every interaction should feel considered rather than defaulted to.

## Design Principles

1. **Restraint is a feature.** Every element earns its presence. If it doesn't serve the user's task, it's noise.
2. **Density respects expertise.** Professionals don't need handheld onboarding. Information should be accessible, not hidden behind progressive disclosure theater.
3. **Dark surfaces, strong hierarchy.** Vercel/Railway-grade aesthetic: dark backgrounds with crisp type and intentional accent use. The surface recedes; the content leads.
4. **Predictable, not surprising.** Consistency in interaction patterns builds trust. Delight through reliability, not unexpected animations.
5. **Performance is design.** Instant response, no spinners for fast operations. Speed is a UI quality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Dark surfaces require careful contrast checks — favor high-contrast text over low-contrast "aesthetic" grays. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any transitions.
